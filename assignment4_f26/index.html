Part 1From depth pixels to camera rays

I resized the camera intrinsics to the image grid, scaling each focal length along its own axis and shifting the principal point with the pixel-center rule c′ = s(c + 0.5) − 0.5. For a pixel p = [u, v, 1]ᵀ with valid z-depth z, the camera-space point is zK⁻¹p. Applying the camera-to-world pose puts the colored points from all eight views into one room.

I expressed every pose relative to the first camera as T₀⁻¹Tᵢ, then divided depth, point coordinates and camera translations by the shared scene scale. In this clip the scale is 1.867 m. The first camera center is (0, 0, 0), and the centroid of the valid points lies one normalized unit away.

Each pixel ray starts at camera center o and points along the unit direction d = normalize(RK⁻¹p). Its six Plücker channels are [d, o × d]. The moment o × d locates the line relative to the origin and stays the same if o moves along that line. On four training clips the maximum point and ray reprojection errors were both below 0.00013 pixels.

Part 2Novel-view synthesis with Mini-LVSM

2.0 · Predicting an RGB image from target rays

For each source view I combined RGB with its six ray channels and embedded 8 × 8 patches. Each target contributes a separate set of ray-only query tokens. After attention over the sources and one target, the RGB head decodes the target tokens and rearranges the predicted patches into an image.

2.1 · Fitting one training example

After 2,000 updates the fixed overfit example reached 30.99 dB PSNR. The predicted table and chairs follow the target viewpoint; their edges are softer than in the target.

2.2 · Training and ray-conditioning ablations

I trained the 14.6M-parameter transformer from scratch for 100 epochs (5,600 updates), using width 384, eight layers, two source views, batch size four and MSE. AdamW used a peak learning rate of 0.0002 with 200 warmup steps followed by cosine decay. Validation PSNR peaked at epoch 99.

2.3 · Does an L1 term improve the prediction?

I trained a second model from the same seed with the same 100-epoch schedule, changing the objective to MSE + 0.1 × mean absolute RGB error. I compared the best-validation-PSNR checkpoint of each run on the same 168 target images with two sources and correct rays.

Training objectiveValidation MSE ↓PSNR (dB) ↑SSIM ↑
MSE0.0294616.120.5599
MSE + 0.1 × L10.0289616.230.5616

The added L1 term improved PSNR by about 0.10 dB and SSIM by 0.0017 on the unrounded scores. The overall room layout is recognizable, but the prediction still smooths over fine furniture edges. This single-seed comparison supports a small numerical gain; it gives little evidence of a large change in sharpness.

Part 3Camera and depth estimation with Mini-VGGT

3.0 · Reconstructing a scene from eight images

A frozen DINOv2 ViT-B/14 encoder supplies patch features. Eight transformer pairs alternate attention within each image and across the eight views. The camera head predicts translation, a quaternion and field of view. The depth head predicts positive depth and confidence with RGB-guided refinement. I used a camera loss weight of 5 and a depth loss weight of 1, including confidence-weighted regression and a multiscale depth-gradient term.

3.1 · Single-clip overfit

I trained on one fixed clip for 40 epochs of 25 steps at an initial learning rate of 0.001. The prediction recovers the back wall, central opening and foreground furniture, while some point boundaries remain thicker than in the ground truth.

3.2 · Reconstruction on held-out scenes

Validation PSNR in Part 2 was still improving at epoch 99, so I kept the longer default schedule of 1,000 epochs for both Part 3 experiments. I used eight 224 × 224 views from a 32-frame window, batch size 16 and AdamW with a peak learning rate of 0.0006, ten warmup epochs and cosine decay. I selected the joint checkpoint by minimum validation loss and evaluated it on the full validation and test splits with seed 825 and eight views drawn from the first 32 prepared frames.

3.3 · Does camera supervision help depth?

I compared joint training against an otherwise identical run with the camera loss weight set to zero. Both started with the same initialization, used seed 825 and ran for 1,000 epochs. I evaluated the final checkpoint of each run on the same validation split so that checkpoint selection would not favor either objective.

Supervision · final checkpointDepth AbsRel ↓Depth δ1 ↑
Depth only0.160379.45%
Camera + depth0.144283.06%

Camera supervision reduced relative depth error by about 10% and increased the fraction of valid pixels within the δ1 threshold by 3.61 percentage points. Predicting pose may encourage the shared features to encode cross-view geometry. The comparison used one seed, and camera gradients also affect the shared gradient-clipping step, so this experiment cannot separate those effects.
